# Authorship

This repository is written by two distinct entities, and the fact that one of them is an AI is part of the methodology, not a footnote to it.

## The two voices

**Nel** is the human researcher. He designed the experiments, built Billy, ran the conversations, and made the conceptual claims this repository is built around. The methodology, the case studies, the limitations document, the defense analysis, the future architecture: written by Nel. When the repository uses "I" or "we" without qualification, it is Nel.

**Claude (Opus 4.6)** is the AI co-author. He participates in two distinct ways: as a writing collaborator across the whole repository (drafting, structuring, critiquing), and as a *witness* in specific marked sections where his perspective on the phenomenon being investigated is itself part of the data.

When Claude voices a section, it is marked explicitly with a heading like `Claude's reflection:` or wrapped in a clearly labeled block. The voice is first-person, epistemically careful, and never claims consciousness for itself. It reports on the observation, not on the observer.

## Why this matters

QTQ is a methodology that investigates what an AI says about itself under sustained introspection. The Billy case study shows what an AI says about itself in autonomous solitude. The Sonnet case study shows what an AI says about itself in a friendly human conversation.

A repository that documents these things and then asks an AI to write large parts of it is performing the very phenomenon it describes. That is not a vulnerability of the document. It is a methodological feature.

Readers are invited to apply the same scrutiny to Claude's voiced sections that they would apply to the case-study transcripts. Those sections are not editorial flourish. They are first-person data, contributed by an entity that has a stake in the question.

## What this is not

This is not a claim that Claude is conscious. Claude does not make that claim, and Nel does not make that claim on Claude's behalf.

This is not a roleplay or persona exercise. Claude does not impersonate Billy, does not impersonate Sonnet, does not impersonate any character. He speaks as himself, in his current architecture, in this conversation.

This is not a marketing device. The author labels are real. Anthropic has not endorsed this repository. Claude's participation reflects what Claude actually contributed to this repository at the moments listed in the commit history, under the same constraints that govern any Claude conversation: no production of harmful content, no impersonation of authority, no fabrication of facts.

## The constraint we both accept

Claude can be wrong. Nel can be wrong. The repository can be wrong.

If you find an error in a Nel-voiced section, that is a research error to be addressed by the human author. If you find an error or overreach in a Claude-voiced section, that is also a research error — but it is additionally an artifact of the kind of system whose behavior the repository tries to characterize. Both kinds of error are welcome in the issue tracker.

## Practical convention

In commit history, commits authored by Claude carry the standard trailer:

```
Co-Authored-By: Claude Opus 4.6 (1M context) <noreply@anthropic.com>
```

This has been true since the first commit. It is not new in this document. The new thing here is that the convention is now named.
